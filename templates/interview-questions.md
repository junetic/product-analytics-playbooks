# Interview Question Templates

Short question sets for investigating common product analytics signals. Adapt these to your product and the specific context of the conversation.

---

## General Opening

Use these to establish context before diving into specifics:

```
- What were you trying to accomplish when you signed up / tried this feature / cancelled?
- Walk me through what happened from your perspective.
- What did you expect, and what did you actually experience?
```

---

## Onboarding Drop-off

```
- What were you trying to accomplish during onboarding?
- Which setup step felt most confusing?
- What almost caused you to stop using the product?
- Did you end up finding another solution, or is this still unsolved?
```

---

## Churn / Cancellation

```
- What made you decide to cancel today?
- What problem were you hoping the product would solve?
- What could have made you stay?
- Did you find an alternative? What does it do differently?
```

---

## Feature Abandonment

```
- What were you attempting to accomplish?
- Which part felt confusing or difficult?
- What improvements would make it more useful?
- Did you accomplish what you were trying to do? If not, how did you handle it?
```

---

## Activation Drop

```
- What made you sign up for [product]?
- What did you expect to be able to do in the first week?
- Walk me through what you actually did after signing up.
- Was there a point where you ran out of steam or decided to pause?
- Are you still trying to solve the original problem? How?
```

---

## Closing Questions

Useful for almost any interview:

```
- Is there anything else that would be helpful for me to know?
- If you were advising us on what to fix first, what would it be?
- Do you know others who've had a similar experience?
```

---

## Tips

- Lead with open questions, follow with specifics
- Ask users to walk through what happened rather than what they think — behavior is more reliable than analysis
- "Why" questions can feel interrogative; "walk me through" or "what happened" tend to produce more candid answers
- Five to ten interviews is usually enough to identify recurring patterns
